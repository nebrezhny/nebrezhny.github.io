📦 What You'll End Up With

  • 😊 A cute animated face. The little screen shows a face that reacts to what is happening — it looks at you when listening, thinks while working, and lights up when it answers.
  • 🔘 One-button operation. There is nothing to learn. One button does the whole job, so anyone in your household can use it.
  • 🔋 A gadget that fits in a pocket. It runs on a small rechargeable battery and lasts through the day with normal use.

🧰 What You Need Before You Start

The whole project is designed for people who have never built electronics before. There is no soldering iron involved, and every part plugs together.

  • A Windows computer running Windows 10 or Windows 11.
  • A USB-C cable that can carry data, not just charge a phone.
  • An ESP32 touchscreen gadget board — the small screen-and-speaker device this project is built for. A Muse Home Link works perfectly, and a comparable board costs about $30.
  • An internet connection, because the gadget asks a cloud service for its answers.
  • About 20 minutes of your time, most of which is waiting for a download.

You do not need to install a programming language, you do not need a developer account, and you do not need to understand what a "firmware" is. Everything is handled for you.

🚀 Getting Started, Step by Step

Follow these steps in order and do not skip ahead. Each one takes only a minute or two.

  1. Open the download link above. Visit this link to download the application, then save the file to your computer.
  2. Plug the gadget into your PC. Use the USB-C cable to connect the small touchscreen device to a free USB port on your computer. The screen should light up. If it stays dark, try a different cable — some cables only charge and cannot send data.
  3. Run the app you downloaded. If Windows shows a blue "Windows protected your PC" box, click More info and then Run anyway. This is normal for small open source tools that are not sold through a big store.
  4. Let the app find your gadget. The app looks for the connected device automatically. When it shows a green tick or the name of your device, you are ready to continue.
  5. Press the setup button. The app copies everything the gadget needs onto it. This takes a few minutes. Do not unplug the cable while the progress bar is moving.
  6. Wait for the restart. The gadget will blink, show a friendly face, and restart itself. That means it worked.
  7. Unplug and try it. Disconnect the cable, hold the button, and say hello. You should hear a spoken answer within a couple of seconds.

🗣️ Your First Conversation

Using the gadget takes about five seconds to learn:

  • Hold the button and wait for the face to change. That means it is listening.
  • Ask your question in a normal speaking voice, from a normal distance. There is no need to shout.
  • Let go of the button when you finish your sentence. The face will switch to a thinking look.
  • Listen to the answer. The gadget speaks the reply out loud and the face animates while it talks.

Try things like "what's the weather like today", "how many tablespoons are in a cup", or "tell me a short joke". Short, clear questions get the best results.

🎨 Make It Your Own

Once the basics work, there is a lot you can change without touching any code. Everything here is optional, so treat it as a bit of fun rather than homework.

  • Change the face. Swap the eyes, the mouth, and the colours so the gadget matches your personality.
  • Pick a different voice. Choose from the voice options included with the Muse Gadget SDK.
  • Give it a personality. Add a short instruction such as "answer briefly and warmly" and the replies will change to match.
  • Adjust the volume. The app includes a simple volume slider if the speaker is too loud or too quiet for your room.

🛠️ Troubleshooting

Most problems come down to one of the items below. Work through them in order.

  • The app cannot find my gadget. Unplug the cable and plug it into a different USB port, ideally one directly on the computer rather than on a hub. Try a different cable too. Charging-only cables are the most common cause.
  • Windows blocked the app from running. Open the file again and choose More info, then Run anyway. You may also need to click Yes on the permission prompt.
  • The screen stays black after setup. Hold the power button for five seconds to force a restart. If it stays dark, reconnect the cable and run the setup step again.
  • It hears me but says nothing back. Check that your computer or phone hotspot is on the same network the gadget was set up with, and that the volume is turned up.
  • Setup stopped halfway through. Unplug the cable, plug it back in, and run the setup again from the beginning. Nothing is permanently broken by an interrupted setup.

💡 Tips for Best Results

  • Speak slightly slower than you would to a person, and pause for a moment after your question.
  • Use it away from a noisy fan or a loud television, because background sound makes listening harder.
  • Ask one thing at a time. Two questions in one sentence often get only one answer.
  • Charge the gadget with the same cable you used for setup. The battery lasts longer when you avoid fast chargers.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to know how to program?

No. The guide is written for people with zero coding experience, and the setup is done by clicking buttons.

Do I need to solder anything?

No soldering at all. Every part plugs in, and the only tool you need is a USB-C cable.

How much does the whole thing cost?

About $30 for the gadget itself. The software, the Muse Gadget SDK, and this guide are all free.

Does it work without internet?

No. The gadget sends your question to a service in the cloud and speaks the answer it gets back, so it needs a connection.

Can I undo the setup?

Yes. Running the setup again replaces what is on the device, and it can always be restored to how it arrived.
